## Where Ideas Die in Most Orgs

Think about where ideas actually live in your organization right now.

A chat thread that scrolled away. A photo of a whiteboard. A "parking lot" slide nobody reopens. A backlog labeled *someday* that everyone has quietly agreed means *never*.

The result is predictable: **the loudest idea wins, not the best one.** Whoever repeats their suggestion most often — or closest to the budget meeting — gets it built. Everything else evaporates.

And the opposite failure is just as common. With no gate between "someone said it" and "someone is building it," teams either build everything and drown, or build nothing and stagnate.

> An idea is not work. An idea is a *candidate* for work — and candidates should have to earn their place.

Every gate on the board exists to enforce that distinction.

## The Phase-Gate Walkthrough

IdeaIQ runs every idea through the same structured journey: **capture → organize → prioritize → implement**. Each phase ends in a gate, and an idea has to earn its passage through every one.

### **Intake**

Everything starts on the board. Any idea, from anyone, gets captured in one shared, real-time place — not in a DM, not in someone's notebook. I made capture as cheap as possible, because the cost of losing a good idea at this stage is invisible and permanent.

### **Organize**

Raw ideas are messy — duplicated, vague, overlapping. This is where the team shapes them: structuring, grouping, and making them comparable. Collaborative by design, because an idea sharpened by five people beats an idea defended by one.

### **Prioritize**

This is the gate that matters most. Ideas compete openly, and most of them should lose. **A phase-gate process that never kills an idea isn't a process — it's a queue.** The ideas that survive here do so on merit, in front of everyone, not because someone escalated in a hallway.

### **Implement**

Only the ideas that cleared every gate reach the far side of the board. By then, they are no longer suggestions — they are decisions, with the context of why they won still attached.

The live site calls IdeaIQ an *Innovation Operating System*, and that is the honest framing: it is not a suggestion box. It is the operating discipline for the fuzzy front end of delivery.

## FAQ

### Is IdeaIQ just a suggestion box with extra steps?

No — a suggestion box collects and forgets. On IdeaIQ, an idea has to keep earning its place: it crosses the board gate by gate, competes openly at each one, and most ideas are supposed to lose. What survives comes out the other side as a decision, not a suggestion.

### How does the phase-gate process in IdeaIQ work?

Every idea in IdeaIQ moves through the same journey — capture, organize, prioritize, implement — with a gate at the end of each phase. Ideas compete openly to pass each gate, so what gets built is decided on merit rather than volume.

### Where does IdeaIQ sit in the IQ suite?

IdeaIQ sits upstream of everything: ideas are explored and prioritized there before StoryIQ turns them into verified, passed stories and SquadIQ runs the delivery boards. Together they cover the pipeline from raw idea to shipped work.
