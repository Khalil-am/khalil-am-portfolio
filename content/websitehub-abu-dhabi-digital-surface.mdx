Ask yourself a simple question: when was the last time you *enjoyed* using a government website? Anywhere in the world, the honest answer usually involves a maze — a different layout on every site, navigation that resets with every department, the same information published three ways in three places.

**WebsiteHub** — the programme I lead product management for at [Ebtikar AI](https://www.ebtikarai.com/) — is Abu Dhabi's move to write a different story. It is a harmonization programme: dozens of government entity websites converging onto one coherent, bilingual, accessible digital surface, so that a citizen moving between entities feels like they never left a single government.

None of the maze happens because anyone wanted it. It happens because every entity builds its own site, in its own era, with its own vendor, for its own reasons — which is why the answer has to be a programme, not another website.

## Why a Government's Web Estate Fragments

Fragmentation is not a failure of effort. It is the natural state of any large organization's web presence left to grow on its own.

Every entity has a mandate, a brand, and a budget cycle. Each one commissions a website when it needs one. Multiply that by decades and dozens of entities, and you get what I call an **accumulated estate**: overlapping technology stacks, inconsistent information architecture, duplicated content with no single source of truth, and accessibility that depends entirely on which team happened to build which site.

The cost lands on three groups:

- **Citizens and residents**, who re-learn every site from scratch and can't always trust that what they read is current.
- **Entity teams**, who each carry the full weight of running a website — infrastructure, security, content operations — alone.
- **Government itself**, which pays many times over for the same capabilities and still can't guarantee a consistent standard.

## What Harmonization Actually Means

"Put everything on one platform" is the headline, but the platform is the *least* interesting part. The real work of WebsiteHub is product work:

**One design system, many identities.** A shared component library and design language that every entity site is assembled from — while each entity keeps its own identity within that system. Consistency and brand are not enemies; a good design system holds both.

**Content as an operation, not an event.** Unified content standards, clear authoring workflows, and governance that defines who writes, who reviews, and who publishes. A website is never "delivered" — it is operated, and harmonization means giving every entity the same strong operating model.

**Accessibility and bilingual parity as defaults.** Accessible, right-to-left-ready, Arabic-first experiences shouldn't depend on which entity you're visiting. When these are built into the shared foundation, every entity inherits them on day one.

**Migration at portfolio scale.** Moving dozens of live websites is a programme in itself — sequencing, entity onboarding, content audits, and go-lives that protect continuity for the people who rely on those sites every day.

## The Public Backdrop: an AI-Native Government

WebsiteHub doesn't exist in a vacuum. Abu Dhabi has publicly committed to one of the most ambitious digital agendas anywhere: the [Abu Dhabi Government Digital Strategy 2025–2027](https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/government-affairs/abu-dhabi-government-launches-digital-strategy-2025-2027/) puts AED 13 billion behind becoming an [AI-native government](https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/technology/abu-dhabi-government-digital-strategy-2025-2027-accelerates-ai-native-government-journey/), and the [TAMM platform](https://www.tamm.abudhabi/) already delivers unified government services at scale.

Websites are where that ambition meets the public eye. Services can be unified behind the scenes, but the web estate is the *face* of government — and when any government's web estate fragments, the face no longer matches the ambition behind it. Harmonizing it is how the digital surface catches up with the digital strategy.

## Designing for the Next Interface: GEO

Here is the part I find genuinely exciting as a product leader: we are harmonizing these websites at the exact moment the way people *find* government information is changing.

Increasingly, a resident doesn't browse to an entity website — they ask an AI assistant, and the assistant reads the web on their behalf. That makes **Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)** a public-service concern, not a marketing trick. Government content now has to be:

- **Structured**, with proper semantics and schema, so machines can parse what a service is, who it's for, and what it costs.
- **Self-contained**, so an answer extracted from a page is still true without the page around it.
- **Consistent**, because when the same fact lives on five sites in four versions, AI engines amplify the confusion at scale.

A harmonized estate is the best possible foundation for this. One source of truth, one content standard, one structured-data layer — and suddenly every entity is legible not just to citizens, but to the AI systems citizens are starting to trust. I practice what I preach: this site's own blog is [optimized for both search engines and AI engines](/blog), down to structured data and an llms.txt.

## What It Takes to Run a Programme Like This

Leading product on a portfolio-scale programme is a different sport from running a single product. A few principles I hold onto, shaped by [years of enterprise consulting](/blog/business-consultant-framework) and [delivery governance](/blog/squadiq-delivery-os):

1. **The portfolio is the product.** Every decision — a component, a content rule, a workflow — is multiplied across dozens of entities. Small quality differences compound into enormous outcomes.
2. **Governance should feel like guardrails, not gates.** Standards only survive if entities can move fast *within* them. The moment governance becomes a queue, teams route around it.
3. **Evidence over opinion.** With this many stakeholders, the only durable arguments are documented requirements, measured baselines, and traceable decisions.
4. **Respect the entity.** Harmonization fails when it flattens. Every entity has real mandates and real users; the shared system has to serve them, not erase them.

## FAQ

### What is WebsiteHub?

WebsiteHub is a harmonization programme unifying dozens of Abu Dhabi government entity websites into one coherent, bilingual, accessible digital experience — a shared design system, shared content standards, and a shared operating model, with each entity keeping its own identity.

### Why does website harmonization matter for citizens?

Because fragmentation taxes everyone: citizens re-learn every site, content drifts out of sync, and quality depends on which entity built which site. A harmonized estate means one familiar experience, one source of truth, and accessibility by default.

### What is GEO, and why does it matter for government websites?

Generative Engine Optimization is the practice of structuring content so AI assistants can find, understand, and cite it accurately. As more residents get answers through AI instead of browsing, machine-readable and consistent government content becomes a public-service requirement.
