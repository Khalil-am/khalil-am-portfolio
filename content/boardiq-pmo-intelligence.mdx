Every PMO I have worked in runs on a board. Cards, columns, labels, due dates. The board is always up — on the wall screen, in the standup, in the weekly review — and everyone feels informed.

Then someone senior asks a real question, and the board goes quiet.

I built **BoardIQ** to close that gap. It is a real-time PMO task intelligence platform that connects to Trello and layers analysis on top of the data the board already holds — **lagging tasks, client priority scores, team workload analytics, and an AI assistant** for instant insights.

---

## The Questions a Kanban Board Can't Answer

Put these three questions to a Trello board:

- **What is actually lagging?** Not "what sits in the Doing column" — what has quietly stopped moving while everything around it kept flowing?
- **Whose plate is full?** Not "who is assigned to cards" — who is carrying so much that the next assignment is a delay wearing a name tag?
- **Which client is at risk?** Not "which cards carry a client label" — which relationship is drifting toward a difficult phone call?

The board cannot say. A kanban board tells you *position* — where a card sits right now. A PMO runs on *motion* — what changed, how fast, and compared to what.

So the PMO compensates the manual way. Someone exports the board, builds a spreadsheet, colors the cells, and circulates a report — which is stale before the meeting it was written for. I made the longer argument against that loop in [Why Every PMO Needs Real-Time Data Intelligence](/blog/pmo-data-intelligence). BoardIQ is what that argument looks like as a product.

## What BoardIQ Layers on Top of Trello

BoardIQ does not ask the team to leave its board. It connects to Trello and turns card activity into a live PMO dashboard — the analysis happens on top, in real time, while the team keeps working where it already works.

Four layers do the heavy lifting.

### Lagging tasks

The first thing a PMO needs is the list nobody has to compile: what has fallen behind. BoardIQ surfaces lagging tasks in real time, so *"what's slipping?"* stops being an investigation and becomes a glance.

### Client priority scores

Boards are organized by task; risk is organized by client. BoardIQ rolls the picture up into **client priority scores**, so the question leadership actually asks — *which client needs attention now* — has an answer the PMO does not have to reconstruct card by card.

### Team workload analytics

"Whose plate is full?" deserves better than a count of assigned cards. BoardIQ's **workload analytics** show how work is actually distributed across the team — which is the difference between reassigning early and apologizing late.

### The AI assistant

Every dashboard answers the questions it was designed for, and the next question is always one nobody designed for. That is the one lesson [building 100+ dashboards in enterprise BI](/blog/enterprise-bi-reporting) never let me forget. BoardIQ ships with an **AI assistant for instant insights**: ask about the board in plain language and get an answer without waiting for anyone to build a report.

That last layer is why the product introduces itself as an *executive decision platform* rather than another chart page. The output that matters is not a visual — it is a decision made earlier than it would have been.
